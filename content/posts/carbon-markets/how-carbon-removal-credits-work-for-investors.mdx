# How Carbon Removal Credits Work for Investors

Carbon removal credits represent a new asset class at the intersection of climate action and alternative investing. Unlike traditional carbon offsets, which focus on avoiding emissions, carbon removal credits fund technologies and practices that actively pull CO₂ from the atmosphere.

## What Are Carbon Removal Credits?

Carbon removal credits (also called carbon dioxide removal or CDR credits) are certificates representing one metric tonne of CO₂ removed from the atmosphere and stored for a verified period. They're typically issued by:

- **Soil carbon programs** where farmers sequester CO₂ through regenerative practices
- **Direct air capture (DAC)** facilities that chemically capture CO₂
- **Biochar** production from agricultural waste
- **Enhanced weathering** using crushed minerals

## How the Market Works

### Supply Side

Project developers (farmers, technology companies, forestry operators) implement carbon removal activities. Third-party verifiers assess the carbon captured, and registries issue credits. Major registries include:

- Puro.earth (engineered and nature-based removals)
- Nori (primarily soil carbon)
- CDR.fyi (aggregator)

### Demand Side

Buyers include:

- **Corporations** meeting net-zero commitments (Microsoft, Shopify, Stripe)
- **Individuals** offsetting personal emissions
- **Speculators** betting on future price appreciation

### Pricing

Prices vary wildly based on:

| Removal Method | Typical Price Range | Permanence |
|----------------|---------------------|------------|
| Soil Carbon | $15-50/tonne | 10-100 years |
| Biochar | $50-150/tonne | 100+ years |
| Direct Air Capture | $400-1000/tonne | 1000+ years |
| Enhanced Weathering | $50-200/tonne | 100,000+ years |

## Investment Thesis

Bulls argue:

1. **Regulatory Tailwinds**: Carbon pricing regimes expanding globally
2. **Corporate Commitments**: Fortune 500 companies need high-quality removals
3. **Supply Scarcity**: High-permanence removals remain expensive and limited
4. **Price Appreciation**: Early adopters may benefit from rising prices

Bears counter:

1. **Additionality Questions**: Would these activities happen anyway?
2. **Verification Challenges**: Measuring soil carbon is imprecise
3. **Permanence Risk**: Stored carbon may be released (fire, tillage)
4. **Regulatory Uncertainty**: No binding framework yet

## Tax Treatment

- **Individuals**: Generally not tax-deductible unless donated to a qualified 501(c)(3)
- **Businesses**: May deduct as ordinary business expense if aligned with operations
- **Form**: Typically receipt or invoice, not a financial instrument

<Callout type="warning">
Consult a tax professional. Treatment varies by jurisdiction and use case.
</Callout>

## Key Takeaways

1. Carbon removal credits fund CO₂ extraction, not just avoidance
2. Prices range from $15 (soil carbon) to $1000+ (direct air capture)
3. Market is voluntary and unregulated, with quality varying widely
4. Retail access is limited but growing
5. Investment thesis hinges on corporate demand and regulatory development

Carbon removal credits may eventually become a recognized asset class, but today they remain speculative and mission-driven.
