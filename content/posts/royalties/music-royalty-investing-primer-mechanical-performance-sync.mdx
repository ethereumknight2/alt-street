# Music Royalty Investing Primer: Mechanical, Performance & Sync Rights

Music royalty investing has emerged as an alternative income strategy, but success requires understanding the three main royalty types: mechanical, performance, and synchronization. Each has different revenue drivers, collection mechanisms, and risk profiles.

## The Three Royalty Types

### 1. Mechanical Royalties

**Source**: Physical sales (CDs, vinyl) and digital streams/downloads

**How They Work**: Every time a song is reproduced—whether pressed onto a CD, downloaded from iTunes, or streamed on Spotify—the songwriter and publisher earn a mechanical royalty.

**Collection**: Via:
- **U.S.**: Harry Fox Agency (HFA), Music Reports
- **Streaming**: Spotify, Apple Music pay directly to distributors
- **Global**: Local mechanical rights societies

**Rates**:
- **Physical/Download**: ~$0.091 per song (U.S. statutory rate)
- **Streaming**: ~$0.0033-0.0054 per stream (varies by platform)

<Callout type="info">
**Example**: A song with 10 million Spotify streams generates ~$33,000-54,000 in mechanical royalties (assuming $0.0033-0.0054/stream), split between songwriter and publisher.
</Callout>

### 2. Performance Royalties

**Source**: Public performance of music (radio, TV, live venues, bars, restaurants, streaming services)

**How They Work**: Whenever a song is played publicly, the songwriter and publisher earn performance royalties. This includes terrestrial radio, satellite radio, and even background music in stores.

**Collection**: Via Performance Rights Organizations (PROs):
- **U.S.**: ASCAP, BMI, SESAC
- **Global**: PRS (UK), SACEM (France), GEMA (Germany), etc.

**Revenue Drivers**:
- Radio airplay (still significant for hit songs)
- Streaming (counted as both mechanical + performance)
- TV broadcasts
- Live performances

**Rates**: Vary by venue, audience size, and medium. PROs collect blanket fees from licensees and distribute proportionally to members.

### 3. Synchronization (Sync) Royalties

**Source**: Music used in visual media (TV shows, films, commercials, video games)

**How They Work**: When a song is "synchronized" with visual content, a one-time licensing fee is negotiated.

**Collection**: Typically negotiated directly between publisher and licensee (e.g., Netflix, Nike, EA Sports)

**Rates**: Wildly variable:
- **Unknown Artist in Indie Film**: $1,000-$10,000
- **Moderate Hit in TV Show**: $10,000-$50,000
- **Major Hit in National Commercial**: $100,000-$1M+

<Callout type="warning" title="Unpredictability">
Sync income is **lumpy and unpredictable**. A song may go years without a sync, then land a Super Bowl ad.
</Callout>

## Which Royalty Type Is Best for Investors?

| Royalty Type | Income Stability | Upside Potential | Predictability |
|--------------|------------------|------------------|----------------|
| **Mechanical** | High (streaming era) | Medium | High |
| **Performance** | Medium (radio declining) | Medium | Medium |
| **Sync** | Low (sporadic) | Very High | Very Low |

**Most Investor-Friendly**: Mechanical royalties from streaming catalogs (predictable, recurring)

**Highest Upside**: Sync royalties (but rare and unpredictable)

## Revenue Mix by Genre

Different genres generate different royalty mixes:

### Pop/Hip-Hop
- **Mechanical**: 60% (streaming dominates)
- **Performance**: 30% (radio still relevant)
- **Sync**: 10% (competitive; many artists vying for placements)

### Country
- **Mechanical**: 40% (streaming + physical still strong)
- **Performance**: 50% (radio remains huge for country)
- **Sync**: 10%

### Classical/Jazz
- **Mechanical**: 30% (niche streaming audience)
- **Performance**: 50% (concert halls, background music)
- **Sync**: 20% (often used in film scores)

## What You're Buying

When you invest in music royalties (via Royalty Exchange, SongVest, etc.), you typically buy:

1. **Songwriter Share**: Income as the songwriter (50% of royalties)
2. **Publisher Share**: Income as the publisher (remaining 50%)
3. **Term**: Often 10-20 years or perpetuity

**Example Deal**:
> *You purchase 100% of songwriter + publisher rights to "Song X" for $100,000. Song generates $10,000/year in royalties (mechanical + performance + sync). Your annual yield = 10%.*

## Income Decay

<Callout type="danger" title="Beware Decay">
Most music catalogs experience **income decay** of 3-7% annually as songs age and cultural relevance fades.
</Callout>

**Exceptions**:
- Holiday classics (evergreen demand)
- Viral resurgence (TikTok effect)
- Classic rock staples (steady radio play)

## Tax Treatment

- **Income Type**: Ordinary income (not qualified dividends)
- **Form**: 1099-MISC (if reported), or self-report
- **Deductions**: Amortize purchase price over useful life
- **Capital Gains**: If you sell rights later

## How Streaming Changed Everything

Pre-2010s, mechanical royalties came from physical sales and downloads. Streaming has:

✅ **Stabilized Income**: Predictable, recurring payments (unlike one-time purchases)

❌ **Lowered Per-Stream Rates**: $0.003-0.005/stream vs $0.091 per download

✅ **Expanded Catalog Value**: Deep catalog now accessible; old songs find new audiences

❌ **Increased Competition**: More music than ever; harder to stand out

## Due Diligence Checklist

Before buying music royalties:

1. **Verify Rights Ownership**: Confirm no disputes, clear chain of title
2. **Review 3+ Years of Statements**: Check trends (growing, stable, declining?)
3. **Understand Revenue Sources**: What % mechanical vs performance vs sync?
4. **Model Decay**: Assume 3-5% annual decline unless proven otherwise
5. **Check PRO/HFA Registration**: Ensure royalties are being collected properly

## Final Thoughts

Music royalties are best viewed as income investments with principal risk. The ideal catalog:

- Generates predictable streaming income (mechanical + performance)
- Has occasional sync upside
- Ages gracefully (holiday songs, classic rock, perennial favorites)
- Is priced at 8-12x trailing income (below that is a deal; above is risky)

Approach with realistic expectations: this is not a growth investment, but a diversified, uncorrelated income stream.
